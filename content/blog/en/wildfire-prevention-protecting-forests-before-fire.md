**Wildfire prevention is one of the most effective ways to protect ecosystems, biodiversity, and the communities that depend on forests.**  
In a context of climate crisis, prolonged droughts, and increasingly frequent extreme events, anticipating fire is no longer optional—it is a responsibility.

### **The Impact of Wildfires**

Wildfires do more than destroy vast areas of forest in a matter of hours. They also generate massive greenhouse gas emissions, degrade soils, damage biodiversity, and put human lives at risk. In many cases, the environmental and social consequences can last for decades.

The good news is that **most wildfires can be prevented**. The key lies in combining planning, technology, on-the-ground work, and community participation.

### **Prevention Starts Before the Fire**

Preventing wildfires means acting long before flames appear. This includes actions such as:

- continuous monitoring of the territory  
- early detection of heat sources  
- controlling access and high-risk activities  
- maintaining firebreaks and strategic areas  
- training local teams and communities  

A preventive approach can significantly reduce the likelihood of wildfires and, when they do occur, **enable faster and more coordinated responses**, minimizing their impact.

### **Technology in Service of Forest Protection**

Today, technology plays a central role in wildfire prevention. The use of satellite imagery, environmental sensors, autonomous drones, and real-time monitoring cameras makes it possible to:

- detect thermal anomalies at an early stage  
- monitor large forest areas continuously  
- anticipate risk scenarios during drought periods  
- improve response capacity in emergency situations  

These tools do not replace work in the field—but they strengthen it by providing accurate information to support timely decision-making.

### **Communities as Key Partners in Prevention**

Effective wildfire prevention is not possible without the people who live in and understand the territory. Working closely with local and Indigenous communities is essential to:

- identify areas with higher fire risk  
- promote responsible fire management practices  
- strengthen local prevention and response capacities  
- build a shared culture of forest protection  

Environmental education, wildfire prevention training, and ongoing dialogue are critical to reducing risks sustainably.

### **Preventing Wildfires Is Climate Action**

Every wildfire prevented means fewer emissions, greater biodiversity protection, and stronger ecosystem resilience in the face of climate change. For this reason, wildfire prevention is not only a conservation strategy—it is **a concrete and high-impact climate action**.

Protecting forests means recognizing that fire is not always inevitable. With planning, technology, community commitment, and a long-term perspective, it is possible to safeguard ecosystems before damage occurs.
